## Continuation candles: engulfing, harami, three white soldiers

Patterns that appear during a pause in a trend and signal that the trend will resume. Reversal candles tell you a move is ending; continuation candles tell you a move is still alive. The same shapes can read as reversal or continuation - the trend context decides, not the shape.

## Key Concepts
- **Bullish engulfing**: two candles. A small red candle is followed by a green candle whose body fully covers (engulfs) the red body. Buyers absorbed all selling and pushed beyond it. Classic textbook use is reversal at a bottom; in an uptrend pullback it is a continuation signal.
- **Bearish engulfing**: mirror image - a green candle followed by a red candle that swallows its body. In a downtrend retracement it confirms sellers resume control.
- **Harami** ("pregnant" in Japanese): a large candle followed by a tiny candle whose body sits entirely inside the first candle's body. The big move stalls. Bullish harami = big red then small green: sellers exhausted. Harami cross (second candle is a doji) is the stronger variant.
- **Three white soldiers**: three consecutive long green candles, each opening within or near the previous body and closing near its high, with little or no upper wick. Sustained buying with no serious resistance. The bearish twin is three black crows.
- **Rising/falling three methods**: the purest continuation setup - one strong candle, a few small counter-trend candles contained inside its range, then a strong candle closing beyond it. Volume shrinks during the middle candles and expands on the final one.
- Direction matters: in an uptrend, bullish continuation sets at the end of pullbacks; in a downtrend, bearish sets at the end of rallies. Reliability is high (studies cite ~70%+) only when trend and volume confirm.

## Reading it on a chart
- First identify the trend (higher highs/lower lows or the 50/200 MA slope), then look for the pattern only inside the pullback, not against the trend.
- Engulfing: at the tail of a pullback, a green body swallows the prior red body - buyers took back the session. Enter on that close, stop below the engulfing candle's low.
- Harami: a tiny candle inside a big body means the correction is running out of sellers; it is weak on its own, so require the NEXT candle to close above the harami's high before acting.
- Three white soldiers: three closes near highs with no long upper wicks, ideally with rising volume on each candle = conviction. Entry after the third candle closes; stop below the first soldier's low.
- Volume is the confirmation: expanding volume on the continuation candle or breakout = real participation; low volume = the pattern is suspect, especially in crypto where thin books fake moves.
- Check the higher timeframe trend and RSI side (above 50 for bullish, below 50 for bearish); confluence with a support level, trendline, or moving average makes the setup much stronger.

## Applying it to signals
- Use continuation candles as a pullback-entry filter: instead of buying a retracement mid-correction and hoping, wait for a bullish engulfing or soldier cluster to print at support, then enter with a tight, logical stop below the pattern. Fewer entries, far fewer knives caught.
- Entries aligned with the trend have a natural edge: the stop is just under the pattern, the target is the next high, so risk is small relative to potential continuation distance (compute R:R before entry).
- Bearish versions (three black crows, bearish engulfing in a downtrend rally) work the same way for shorts and for exiting longs early instead of holding through the resumption of the down move.
- Filter signals: if a continuation pattern prints but volume is flat or RSI is already >70, downgrade or skip it - the move may be exhausted rather than continuing.
- Combine with the previous lessons: a hammer or pin bar at support followed by a bullish engulfing = reversal warning plus confirmation, a high-probability long entry.

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating engulfing/harami as always-reversal: at extremes they flip meaning; only trade them in the direction of the trend unless you have a separate reversal thesis.
- Chasing three white soldiers after a big extension: the pattern is already 3 strong candles; buying the 4th or 5th candle is buying overbought, the classic late-entry trap.
- Ignoring volume: patterns on low volume are the market action of the few, and in crypto they are frequent false wicks.
- Taking the harami alone: it is a stall, not a signal; without the following confirmation candle it fails constantly.
- Missing nearby resistance: a perfect soldier run straight into a major resistance level often stalls there - check the target before entering.
- Wrong stop: stops belong below the pattern's low (or first candle's low), not inside it; wicks get wicked again, so size so the stop distance is affordable.
