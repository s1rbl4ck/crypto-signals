# Market Structure: HH/HL Uptrend, LL/LH Downtrend, Structure Breaks

## Key Concepts
- **Market structure** = the sequence of swing highs and swing lows that tells you *who is in control*: buyers or sellers. It answers "is this trend up, down, or ranging?" better than any single indicator.
- **Swing high (HH/HL reference)** = a local peak where price falls away; **swing low** = a local trough where price bounces.
- **Uptrend = Higher Highs (HH) + Higher Lows (HL).** Each peak and each trough is higher than the last. Bulls keep stepping up.
- **Downtrend = Lower Highs (LH) + Lower Lows (LL).** Each peak and trough is lower. Bears keep pushing down.
- **Ranging / sideways** = highs and lows staying roughly equal (double tops around the same high, double bottoms around the same low). No clear control.
- **Break of Structure (BOS)** = price breaks the most recent swing high in an uptrend (or swing low in a downtrend). Structure continues in the same direction.
- **Change of Character (CHoCH)** = price takes out a structure point that *flips* the trend (e.g. takes the last HL in an uptrend and closes below it) — the first warning the trend may be turning.
- **Role flip / S/R flip** applies here too: a broken swing high often becomes support on a retest, a broken swing low becomes resistance.
- **Timeframe matters:** structure is defined relative to the timeframe you're trading. A swing on the 1H is noise on the daily.

## Reading it on a chart
- Mark the last few obvious swing highs and lows by eye (or use a fractal/zig-zag tool). Connect the pattern: are the lows rising in a row? → uptrend.
- **Confirms an uptrend** when price takes out the prior swing high (BOS) *and* pulls back to a higher low, then pushes on. The higher-low pullback is the confirmation — a lone new high with no follow-through can just be a spike.
- **Confirms a downtrend** analogously: a lower high after taking out the prior swing low, then a push lower.
- **Trend change signal (CHoCH):** in an uptrend, price breaks *below* the last higher low and closes there → the prior higher-low sequence is gone. Wait for the break AND a close/retest, not just an intraday wick.
- Look at a higher timeframe for the "bias" and the current timeframe for clean structure points to trade off.
- A fresh, un-retested structure point (e.g. the most recent higher low) is more meaningful than a distant, already-broken one.

## Applying it to signals
- **Filter trades by trend:** only take longs when you can label HH+HL on the higher timeframe, only take shorts on LL+LH. This kills the classic "trading against the dominant trend" mistake.
- **Entry trigger:** don't buy a breakout blindly — wait for the pullback to the last HL (in an uptrend) or the retest/CHoCH confirmation. Buying the BOS with no confirmation gets you stopped out on fakeouts.
- **Exit/trail logic:** place stops below the most recent structure point (last HL in a long, last LH in a short). Trail the stop up as each new HL forms — this locks profit and lets winners run.
- **Reversal signal:** treat a CHoCH (break of the trend-defining point with a confirmed close) as the early warning to flatten counter-trend longs before the move down, even if RSI hasn't printed divergence yet.
- **Combine with prior lessons:** structure gives you the "what side to be on" (topic 5 trendlines, topic 4 S/R) and RSI/volume add late-confirmation; structure decides the bias, oscillators time the entry.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Labeling structure before it completes** — a swing high isn't confirmed until price actually turns down; drawing it as it's still forming leads to false reads.
- **Trading against the higher-timeframe structure** just because the lower timeframe looks "due for a pullback." HTF bias wins.
- **Chasing every BOS** without waiting for a pullback/retest — most breakouts fake out at first.
- **Ignoring closes** — a wick through a swing low isn't a break; require a candle close beyond the point (or a retest hold) before trusting it.
- **Treating a CHoCH as instant reversal** — it's a *warning*; the real confirmation is the new lower-low/higher-high sequence, not a single candle.
- **Switching timeframes mid-analysis** — be explicit about which timeframe defines the structure or you'll argue with yourself.
