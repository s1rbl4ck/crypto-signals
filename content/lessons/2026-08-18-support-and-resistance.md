## Support and resistance: how levels form, role flipping

The two most important lines on any chart. Support is a price zone where demand (buyers) overwhelms supply, so selling stalls and price bounces. Resistance is where supply (sellers) overwhelms demand, so rallies stall and price pulls back. They are self-fulfilling: because thousands of traders watch the same levels, orders cluster there and make them hold. Nothing magical, just psychology plus order flow.

## Key Concepts
- **How levels form**: repeated bounces at the same price, prior swing highs/lows, round numbers (e.g. 100k BTC, whole-dollar alt prices), high-volume nodes from the past, and dynamic levels like moving averages and trendlines.
- **Strength factors**: more touches = stronger (2-3 minimum, more is better); levels formed after steep moves are stronger; zones with heavy volume are stronger; higher timeframe levels (weekly/monthly) beat lower timeframe ones.
- **Levels are zones, not lines**: buy/sell pressure spreads over a range, so treat a support/resistance area (e.g. +/-0.5-1%) as the level, not one exact tick.
- **Role flipping**: after a decisive break, old resistance becomes new support and old support becomes new resistance. Breakout buyers hold through the retest; traders who shorted at resistance cover there; the crowd that missed the breakout buys the dip at the old level.
- **Breakout confirmation**: a break only counts when it closes beyond the level (candle close, not wick) with rising volume, then holds a retest of the flipped level.

## Reading it on a chart
- Mark zones before price gets there, not after. Find the last 2-3 times price turned at the same area, draw a rectangle around the reaction range, and note the volume at that price.
- A touch that leaves a long lower wick (hammer/pin bar) at support or upper wick at resistance is a rejection: the zone is being defended, ideal with declining volume into the touch.
- In an uptrend, price should find support at the zone and trendline/MA confluence; test counts of 3+ touches make the level "round-trip" reliable for range bounces.
- Watch the break: a strong close beyond the level with volume expansion signals a real breakout. Then wait for the retest: price returns to the broken level and bounces off it from the other side. That flip is the highest-quality confirmation entry.
- If price plunges straight through with no retest and no volume, it is often a false break or liquidity sweep: watch for a fast reclaim (close back inside the zone) before trusting direction.

## Applying it to signals
- Buy at support, not mid-range. In an uptrend, wait for price to reach a tested support zone, confirm with a bullish rejection candle (ties to lessons 1-2), then enter with a stop just below the zone and a target at the next resistance.
- Use resistance as the target and trailing line: take partial profits at resistance instead of hoping price blows through; in ranging markets, fade the same zone for shorts.
- Breakout signals get stricter: require a closing break plus volume, then enter on the retest of the flipped level with a stop back inside the broken zone. This filters most fake breakouts.
- Role-flip levels beat fresh ones: a broken resistance retested as support carries the memory of buyers who missed the break, giving the entry a natural pool of stop-loss fuel beneath it (tight stop, big R:R).
- Confluence is king: a support zone that also lines up with a trendline, the 50 EMA, and a prior week's high is far more reliable than a lone line; grade signals by how many independent factors stack at the level.

## Common Pitfalls
- Drawing a line instead of a zone: price is 1% above your exact line and you miss, or you get wicked out by noise inside the band. Use rectangles, not razor lines.
- Catching falling knives: buying support in a downtrend without a reversal candle or confluence is fighting supply; one more strong close below the zone invalidates it.
- Ignoring false breaks: wicks poking below support are common liquidity sweeps that stop out late sellers and then reverse; judge breaks by closes, volume, and retests.
- Hiding stops right at the obvious level: everyone sees support, so the level gets swept first. Put stops below the zone by the average wick length, or half a zone-width under it.
- Overloading the chart: 20 levels means no level; keep the ones with 2+ touches or strong confluence and delete the rest.
- Holding stale levels in strong trends: a support that held in a range means nothing once trend accelerates; levels decay with time, re-mark them as price moves on.
