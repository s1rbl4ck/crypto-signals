## Reversal candlesticks

Doji, hammer, shooting star, pin bar. One candle shape is never a trade by itself, but it is the earliest warning that a trend is losing steam at a key level.

## Key Concepts
- **Doji**: open and close nearly equal, body tiny or absent. Buyers and sellers are in a standoff. Variants: dragonfly (long lower wick, bullish-leaning), gravestone (long upper wick, bearish-leaning), long-legged. Signals indecision, not direction; it matters where it appears.
- **Hammer**: small body at the top, lower wick at least 2x the body, little to no upper wick. Appears after a DOWNTREND; sellers pushed price down but buyers pulled it back. Bullish reversal warning.
- **Shooting star**: small body at the bottom, upper wick at least 2x the body, little to no lower wick. Appears after an UPTREND; buyers pushed price up but sellers rejected it. Bearish reversal warning.
- **Pin bar**: the umbrella term in price-action trading for any candle with a long wick ("pin") and small body. Bullish pin bar at support = hammer; bearish pin bar at resistance = shooting star. The wick is the story: it marks where price was rejected.
- **Inverted hammer**: same shape as a shooting star but appears after a downtrend, so it reads as a bullish signal (seller exhaustion), not bearish. Context decides meaning, not shape.
- **Reversal is not automatic**: these are early warnings. Confirmation = the next candle closing beyond the pattern's high/low, ideally with volume.

## Reading it on a chart
- Scan the LAST candle after a prolonged move: a doji at an extreme after a long run = momentum pause, first sign of exhaustion.
- Find rejection wicks near obvious levels: a hammer sitting on a support line or a previous low, a shooting star poking into resistance or a previous high. The wick shows who got trapped (buyers above a shooting star's wick, sellers below a hammer's wick).
- Confirm with the NEXT candle: close above the hammer's high = bullish confirmation; close below the shooting star's low = bearish confirmation. A doji as the confirmation candle weakens the signal.
- Volume adds weight: high volume on the reversal candle or its confirmation suggests real institutional participation; low volume in a quiet market produces false wicks.
- Longer wicks = stronger signal (3-5x body is stronger than 2x), and a hammer closing green (close > open) is stronger than a red one.

## Applying it to signals
- Turn wick rejections at known support/resistance into entries with a built-in stop: place the stop just beyond the wick's extreme (below the hammer's low, above the shooting star's high). That is a natural, tight invalidation point.
- Use the reversal candle as a TIMING filter: even if a level looks good, wait for the rejection candle to form instead of buying in the middle of the move. It avoids catching falling knives at support or tops at resistance.
- Compute reward-to-risk BEFORE entry: if the distance to the next resistance/stop is less than 2x the stop distance, skip the trade even if the pattern looks perfect.
- In ranging markets, hammer at range low and shooting star at range high can be traded repeatedly; in strong trends, treat them as noise unless they appear at a major level.
- If a candle looks like a hammer in an uptrend or a shooting star in a downtrend, read it as the opposite meaning (inverted hammer / hanging-man) or ignore it entirely.

## Common Pitfalls
- Trading the pattern with no confirmation; a single candle is a warning, not a command.
- Buying every hammer in a downtrend; hammers only matter at support or after a real decline, and in low volume they are common false signals.
- Ignoring context: in a strong trend, wicks are just volatility, not reversals.
- Stop too tight inside the wick: wick lows get wicked again; stop belongs beyond the extreme, and size the position so that distance is acceptable.
- Confusing shooting star with inverted hammer: same shape, opposite meaning depending on the preceding trend.
- Assuming the reversal is long-term; these patterns often only trigger a pullback, and targets should be set at nearby levels, not "to the moon".
